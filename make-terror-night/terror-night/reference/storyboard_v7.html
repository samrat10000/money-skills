Night Terror: Dead Roads · Episode 1 · teardown of v6 and the v7 plan

Why v6 felt off, and the new episode

I went through v6 frame by frame, second by second, and listened to every line. Below is everything I found wrong, with the actual frame from v6 as proof. After that comes the new episode, with the missing scenes added, so the story flows from the phone ringing to the empty car at the end.

The four root causes

1. I repeated mistakes you had already flagged

Five v6 shots are the same v5 shots you asked me to fix: the blurry Crawler close-up, the skid that freezes and stops, the "potato" Brute, the hollow Stalker mouth, and the flickering headlights. I also replaced the v5 garage reveal, the scene you called the best one.

2. Three different looks in one episode

The wake-up uses a real orange photo of New York, and the ending phone is a photoreal product shot. Everything else is our drawn 3D world with ink lines. The camcorder filter then blurred those ink lines, which are the signature of our style.

3. The story skips the steps between scenes

He never leaves the apartment, takes no keys, has no reason to use the stairs, and there's no lobby. He's never shown getting into the car, and nobody puts the phone on the seat. So the ending phone feels like it's from another film.

4. Sound was built for the music clock, not for people talking

16 lines were squeezed into 52 seconds and placed on the v5 music hits. Four lines overlap, five are buried under score or SFX, and the score plays under every word. The first sound you hear is a fake synthetic buzz plus a music hit.

Dialogue and sound audit

Lines on top of each other

"It's like four in the—" (1.9–4.9 s) overlaps Akash's "Look outside" (3.0–7.0 s) by 1.9 s. Sam's "oh my God" (5.0–6.5 s) is buried inside Akash's line. "Akash? Akash!" (9.3 s) plays over the Screamer's scream. "Please start" runs into "keys, keys".

Lines buried under music

The radio broadcast, "Watch me", "No no no" and "Akash, pick up" all sit under the full chase score and its SFX. The phone call itself has the score running under it the whole time.

One voice for two people

Akash and the radio announcer use the same ElevenLabs voice (Adam), so the emergency radio sounds like Akash.

That opening sound

The "phone buzz" was a synthetic tone I generated, not a real phone, and it sat on top of the score's opening horror hit. A real ringtone, a vibration rattling on wood, rain and a far siren should be the only sounds.

The score was written for a different cut

The Flow track's hits were composed for the v5 picture (the Crawler open). In v6 they land on unrelated shots in the first act and fight the conversation.

Promised in the v6 plan, but not delivered

  • The emergency alert on the phone, and Sam grabbing his keys.
  • The camera move that shows how big the horde is (your v5 note).
  • The garage with tube lights and headlights switching on only once.
  • The aerial of the burning city, the low tyre-cam pass and the zombie on the hood.
  • The smooth drift, Brute v2 with the engine growl, and the Stalker's stitches tearing.
  • The friend's call as a real cliffhanger.

The v7 idea: the camcorder never stops recording

Sam left his camcorder on a tripod at the glass, filming a night timelapse of the skyline. That's why the camera is already rolling when the phone rings, and why it catches the eruption. He grabs the camcorder and keeps filming down the stairs. In the car he props it on the dash, and it keeps recording after he's gone. So the last shot (the phone ringing on an empty seat, the door open) is the dashcam's view, and it finally makes sense.

This also keeps Sam's face off camera without it looking like we're hiding it: he's in silhouette, from behind, his hands, or behind the lens. References: Cloverfield (NYC, a camcorder that keeps recording, the stairwell escape), [REC] (a camera light in a dark apartment stairwell, sound before sight), and The Last of Us opening (a night phone call while the city breaks, then the escape by car).

One look for every shot

Our 3D pencil-sketch style everywhere

  • Ink outlines on every new set and prop, the same treatment the creatures and the car have.
  • One palette: cold blue night, sodium orange for fire and street lamps, red only for blood and eyes.
  • The New York photo is re-painted into the drawn style, and the fire is drawn-style fire, not a glowing oval.
  • The phone and the car interior get the ink treatment too, so the ending matches.

Camera and finish

  • All finals render at 1080x1920 on your Asus, with no low-res cloud renders.
  • The camcorder feel comes from the camera work (handheld, late reframes, step-synced walking) and a light grain. The heavy blur is gone, so the ink lines stay sharp.
  • Sam's face stays out of frame: silhouette, back, hands or POV.

Sound rules

  • No score until the ignition. Before that it's real sound: the ringtone, the room, rain, sirens, footsteps, breathing, the strobe ticking and a heartbeat sub.
  • One line at a time, with at least 0.3 s of breath between lines. Phone voices get a tinny filter.
  • The radio gets a robotic emergency-alert voice. The score dips under every line.

Build order and honest timing

  • Part A (acts 1 and 2, the new 53 s): the penthouse, fire, Screamer eyeline, corridor, stairwell, lobby, garage and car interior. About a day of building and Asus rendering, then a preview.
  • Part B (acts 3 and 4): the aerial, tyre cam, drift, Brute v2, Stalker mouth and the empty-seat ending. About another day.
  • One contact sheet per finished shot, and renders queue behind the teaser on the Asus.
